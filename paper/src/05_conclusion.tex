\section{Conclusion}

This paper presents path-consistency, a model-agnostic prefix-guided self-consistency method for token-efficient test-time reasoning. Instead of generating every sampled branch from scratch, path-consistency extracts high-confidence partial reasoning paths from early samples and reuses them as prefixes to guide subsequent generations. This reduces redundant token generation while preserving the multi-path sampling structure of self-consistency. Experiments on arithmetic, commonsense, and symbolic reasoning benchmarks show that path-consistency reduces inference latency and token consumption while largely maintaining the accuracy of standard self-consistency. These results suggest that reusing intermediate reasoning prefixes is a practical way to improve the efficiency of test-time compute.
